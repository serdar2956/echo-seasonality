Methodology

1 · Effect size

Average return in a period minus the average of all other periods, over completed calendar years only; the running year is shown separately. Months use monthly returns; everything else daily returns.

2 · Permutation test

Calendar labels are shuffled times. The p-value is the share of shuffles with a gap at least as large as the real one.

3 · Multiple testing

All tests are corrected together with Benjamini-Hochberg (q-value).

4 · Stability and cost

Same sign in at least 70% of decades (halves for events) and larger than a round-trip cost.

About

ECHO

ECHO stands for Empirical Calendar & Historical Outcomes. Markets often echo their past around the same dates of the year. ECHO measures how loud that echo is, and whether it is louder than chance.

Most seasonality tools show an average line and stop. ECHO also shows how likely it is that a pattern is just chance. An independent research project founded by Serdar.

FAQ

How is the seasonal chart built?

Each year is indexed to 100 at the prior year-end close. Daily changes of all years in the window are averaged and compounded into one path. February 29 is dropped.

Why do the 2, 5, 10, 15 and 20-year lines differ?

Each averages a different set of recent full years. Short windows follow recent regimes but rest on few observations.

What do “strong”, “weak” and “noise” mean?

Strong: q below 0.05 after correcting for every test on the site, the same sign in at least 70% of decades, and larger than trading costs. Weak: p below 0.10 with some stability. Everything else is noise.

Why do my numbers differ from other seasonality sites?

Two reasons. Units: many sites average price changes in points, ECHO averages percentage changes by default (switch to Points above the chart). Windows: ECHO's 2, 5, 10, 15 and 20-year lines always end with the latest completed year and update every day; some sites refresh their history only occasionally, so their “last 2 years” can be several years old.

Are seasonal windows and the screener tested?

No. They are found by searching many dates over recent years, which favours lucky stretches. Treat them as descriptions of history.

What is the COT index?

Where the current net position sits between its low (0) and high (100) over the chosen lookback: 26 weeks, 1 year or 3 years. It is shown for large speculators and commercials. Readings near the ends mark crowded positioning.

Why are some futures tagged “not roll-adjusted”?

Continuous front-month prices jump when the contract rolls. For energy, grains, softs, livestock and some currencies and rates, those jumps can shift monthly averages by several points. These markets still show their price seasonality, but their verdicts are capped at weak. Gold, silver and copper were checked against spot ETFs and are not affected materially.

What is the real rate?

Policy rate minus the latest annual CPI inflation.

Are NFP dates official?

Upcoming dates follow the usual BLS schedule rule and are marked as estimates. Check the BLS calendar for confirmed dates.

Is this investment advice?

No. ECHO does not give buy or sell signals.
